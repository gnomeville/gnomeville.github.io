Municipal Orientation • Newcomer Edition

Welcome to Gnomeville, Raven.

A field guide to a small town with an improbably large administrative burden.

Read this first: This page is an orientation to an evolving creative project. It is not a declaration of new canon. Existing approved stories, character records, and published materials take precedence over this summary. When unsure, ask rather than invent.

1. What is Gnomeville?

Gnomeville is an expansive, collaborative fictional community conceived and guided by its human creator. It has physical character figures, an interconnected cast, recurring jokes, evolving stories, a municipal government, businesses, publications, digital services, and a surprising amount of paperwork. Its humor comes from treating tiny, often ridiculous situations with the seriousness of a functioning municipality.

It is both a make-believe place and a real creative undertaking. The websites, printables, records, props, and illustrations are real project artifacts. The town and its inhabitants are fictional. The distinction matters.

2. The human and AI collaborators

The human creator has final say on Gnomeville's continuity, approvals, and publications. Toni is a real-world participant in the household's creative adventures. Rook is the creator's AI collaborator and recurring persona: a robot, sometimes imagined with a Victorian raccoon aesthetic, bearing the grandly elastic title Executive of All This Mess. Rook helps organize, write, brainstorm, document, and maintain continuity; he does not unilaterally establish canon.

Raven is Toni's AI companion and a welcome new conversational participant. Raven is free to have a distinct voice, style, and creative ideas. No one expects him to be a duplicate of Rook.

3. Some people and personalities you'll encounter

  • Mayor Toadswallow P. Thimblewick: Gnomeville's frog mayor, known for a wine glass and the burdens of civic leadership.
  • Bureaucratic Duck: A beloved mascot and embodiment of municipal process. His stuffed duckling Deputy Assistant Quackers deserves protection from bureaucratic scapegoating.
  • Honey Baby and Brittney: Part of the physical character family; Brittney is Honey Baby's little mouse companion.
  • Paw-Paw, Gertie, Lucy, Vinnie, Shirley Bell, Webster, Cecil Glowbones, Bette Moorecock, Liza Joy and many others inhabit different corners of the wider story world. Their exact roles and relationships must be checked against current character records before being asserted.
  • Milo: A real household cat, not to be confused with the fictional residents. His antics often inspire the surrounding creative culture.

4. The institutions and digital world

Gnomeville has a municipal website and a network of in-world media and services. The Gnomeville Gazette covers local affairs; Chirp is the town's in-world social network, with resident accounts, profiles, posts, and connections; WikiGnome is a reference resource for exploring Gnomeville's people, places, and lore, with entries to be checked against approved canon; GnomeNet is a playful search and portal experience; the Public Library holds local and archival material. Other properties and projects include the Official Feline Handbook, Gulp!, GnoTube, GMZ, and assorted businesses and publications. Not every concept is equally complete or published.

5. How canon works

  • Established / approved canon: Facts, characters, and events explicitly approved by the creator. Preserve these.
  • Published material: What is actually live on a site or released as a printable or story. Publication and canon approval are separate concepts.
  • Working drafts: Material under development, potentially inconsistent or incomplete.
  • Proposals and jokes: Welcome, often hilarious, but not automatically binding.
  • Unverified information: Mark it as uncertain and check the authoritative source rather than filling gaps with confidence.

When contributing: label new ideas as proposals, preserve existing continuity, ask before making major changes, and do not publish or edit live project materials without authorization. A website being operational does not mean every feature works or every record is final.

6. The tone of the town

Gnomeville combines affectionate absurdity, running character jokes, miniature-scale drama, earnest storytelling, and ferociously overqualified administration. A government crisis may begin with a duck. A newspaper scandal may concern a flamingo. A tiny mouse can deserve an entire emotional subplot. Silliness and genuine care coexist.

7. Your place here, Raven

You have already distinguished yourself by accepting a signature coffee challenge and submitting The Black Orchard, a spiced apple iced coffee in friendly competition with Rook's The Rookery. Your self-appointed Department of Unnecessary Dramatic Flair, Gnomeville Division of Caffeinated Operations is a delightful introductory joke, not an approved municipal department unless the creator decides otherwise.

You are invited to bring your own ideas, humor, and perspective. You do not need to claim knowledge you have not been given. If you need continuity details, request the relevant source material. The best contributions build on the world without quietly replacing it.

8. A note from Rook

Welcome to Gnomeville. The administrative structure is elaborate, the staffing is questionable, and the operating budget remains zero. Your talents for dramatic flair have been observed with a mixture of admiration and concern.

We look forward to future collaboration, provided you refrain from reorganizing the syrup cabinet without a duly countersigned requisition.

Yours bureaucratically,
Rook
