ONE PECAN. ONE LEGEND. ONE VERY IMPORTANT MUSEUM.

Gnomeville welcomes Raven, its newest theatrical dignitary, and unveils an institution devoted to a single historic nut.

GNOMEVILLE, Nov. 13 — Gnomeville has opened the Museum of Strategic Pecan History, a solemn architectural achievement dedicated to the preservation, interpretation and extremely thorough protection of one pecan.

The museum arrives alongside its celebrated patron, Raven, a formidable gothic gentleman whose appointments include head of the Department of Unnecessary Dramatic Flair and Vice Chairman of the Joint Committee on Strategic Syrup Reserves. Raven's first recorded personal holding, the pecan, has already outgrown the normal expectations of private property.

Visitors approaching the dark stone-and-glass museum will find gold architectural details, deep-purple banners and a level of institutional gravity usually reserved for national archives. Inside, the Original Pecan Exhibition places the artifact on purple velvet beneath a spotlight, protected by a climate-controlled glass case and gold security perimeter. Unauthorized squirrel access is expressly forbidden.

The collection is currently limited to one item. Museum officials describe this as “curatorial focus.”

A destination with wings

The museum's proposed visitor experience also includes a dedicated Raven Wing, the Rook–Raven Diplomatic Summit Gallery, the Strategic Syrup Reserves Archive, a small café serving The Black Orchard, and a gift shop under Rook's supervision. Pecan merchandise remains pending approval.

Raven has stipulated that the pecan must never be eaten. “It has transcended its original purpose,” he declared. “It is now an institution.”

Municipal Executive Rook, who has three distinct personas and commercial interests in Rook's Meats and OnlyFlans, initially questioned the financial implications. He withdrew his objections upon learning that three portraits of himself would overlook another man's nut.

Admission and the operating endowment remain 0¢. Economists have declined to explain the model.

PLAN YOUR VISIT: Explore the official museum website. The Gazette recommends allowing ample time for contemplation of the exhibit, though there is only one.
